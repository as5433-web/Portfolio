ELT-213 / Active Circuit Design / Team project

Self-playing guitar amplifier

A guitar amplifier that mixes a live instrument with a tunable drone note, giving the player a sustained tone to play alongside.

Design & construction

Our team combined an LM386 amplifier, an XR2206 function generator, and a passive summing circuit to create this project. The XR2206 IC replaced the originally planned ICL8038 IC after a shipping delay. The report documents a tunable drone note range of approximately 100 to 3,000 Hz.

Each module was built and tested separately before being integrated. The finished system was mounted on an open-faced 3D-printed plate, with routed wiring and accessible controls for volume, gain, mixing, and frequency.

Troubleshooting & refinement

Unwanted distortion, unequal signal levels, and ground-looping hum required several revisions. The oscillator measured close to 8V peak-to-peak, so simply pairing it to the guitar signal did not produce a balanced mix. We removed the second amplifier stage, added a resistive summing network, and adjusted the controls to isolate and level-match the inputs.

The final build powered the LM386 from a 9V battery and the oscillator from a separate 12V supply. A foot pedal changed the tuning resistance to switch between two preset drone notes, and a toggle switch selected either speaker or headphone output.

Final result

Final testing recorded a clear adjustable drone note, clean guitar amplification, impressive distortion, and reliable floor pedal switching. The project brought together circuit design, soldering, oscilloscope measurements, mechanical layout, and iterative troubleshooting.

ELT-210 / Electronic Fabrication / Team project

Electronic calculator

An Arduino-based calculator built as a complete physical device, integrating a keypad, LCD, circuit board, and 3D-printed enclosure.

Hardware & assembly

I coordinated a four-person team through the fabrication project. The report documents an Arduino Uno SMD R3 as the controller, a matrix keypad for input, an LCD for output, a contrast-adjustment potentiometer, and a battery-powered supply with an on/off switch.

The work connected schematic and layout preparation with breadboard prototyping, component assembly, soldering, Arduino program setup, and enclosure integration. A 3D-printed PLA housing brought the controls and electronics together into a handheld unit.

Challenges & lessons

The team had to correct schematic errors, adapt to delayed parts, and modify keypad and switch connections for assembly. We also corrected a program-upload issue and investigated the prototype's sensitivity to low battery voltage.

The report documents the assembled calculator and its internal wiring. The main lesson of this project was that a working design depends on coordinating the electrical connections, software setup, power source, and mechanical fit throughout the build.

Arduino rover project

A robotics team project combining mechanical assembly, Arduino hardware, and MATLAB programming.
